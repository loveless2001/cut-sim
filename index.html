The Cut

Operational core, quantum realization, and scoped experiments — working framework v0.5.

Framing question. How much of a physical model’s causal structure can an embedded observer reconstruct from the signals, interventions, and records actually available to it? The operational core admits primitive local sequencing. A declared realization adds dynamics and interventions, yielding model-relative causal accessibility and reachability. Calibrated clocks give duration; history asymmetry and records concern the arrow; record-based inference supports temporal judgement. These layers require additional assumptions and do not uniquely fix ontology.

The layers connect by adding assumptions and testing consequences. A realization or interpretation can fail without refuting the whole operational core.

Four time-related layers

LayerQuestionScope
Causal accessibility → orderingWhich events can affect which others?Interventions and dynamics; incomparable events may remain unordered.
Calibrated clocks → durationHow much physical change accumulates?Requires read/count rules and comparison; order supplies no seconds.
History asymmetry + records → arrowWhy do records and histories favor a direction?Boundary conditions, entropy, persistence, loss, and recoverability.
Record-based inference → temporal judgementHow does an observer infer before/after and now?Memory, integration, prediction, and report.

How to read the claims

Every claim in the spec carries a status tag. Use the tags to distinguish established results, assumptions, conjectures, postulates, and limitations:

EST — established result in the literature; not original here.

FIELDS — due to Chris Fields and collaborators; assembled, not originated, by this framework.

ASM — assembly: established pieces combined, possibly original in arrangement; no new mathematics.

CONJ — conjecture: plausible, not proved, flagged open.

POST — postulate: a philosophical commitment of the ontology, adopted rather than derived.

GAP — a declared limitation, missing bridge, or scope boundary; a no-go only where separately proved.

Two unresolved inputs

The current realization tracks two unresolved inputs, historically called “the two turtles.” This is bookkeeping for this construction, not a claim about every possible theory:

Arrow conditions GAP

Unitarity gives reversible evolution; time-reversal symmetry is a separate dynamical condition. A macroscopic arrow requires additional boundary/preparation, environmental, and coarse-graining assumptions. The orientation experiment found a scoped null for a decoupled complement in its declared access class; it is not a universal corollary.

Cut selection GAP

The present input tuple does not supply a basis-independent natural rule selecting a preferred factorization ℋA ⊗ ℋB. A richer physical theory may add locality or observable structure; any broader no-selection claim needs its own assumptions and proof.

Why the simulations are included

A generic lattice has a direction-dependent maximum signal speed, while Lorentz invariance requires isotropic measured propagation in its domain. The ruler-cancellation proposal was therefore tested in an exact anisotropic free-fermion simulation with endogenous rods and clocks and preregistered verdict rules. The result rejected the general cancellation claim, identified a square-root scaling law, and supported cancellation only for the tested infrared observables. See the Verdict Lab.

The second experiment asks whether relative orientation or purification can be distinguished in a two-sided fermion thermofield-double model with no cross-cut coupling and the declared side-R procedures. The internal battery remained null at float precision (10⁻¹⁵), while model-level controls detected the difference. In the tested crossover, orientation entered at second order in g and the complement micro-state at first order. The result does not cover arbitrary couplings, access classes, or ontologies. See the Orientation Lab.
