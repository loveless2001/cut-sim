The Cut — Operational Core and Limits of Inference

Proposed foundation for this research programme · version 0.5 · 2026-09-23

Role. This module states the conditions under which a system can distinguish, record, compare, and test models from within its actual range of access. It is not a new definition of time, not a replacement for the ontology of The Cut, and not a denial of reality outside an observer.

Status. The commitments below are a proposed basis. They have not been proved to be the unique minimal axioms or fully logically independent. An observer may be any physical record-capable system; consciousness is not assumed.

Three layers

The layers are related by adding assumptions and testing consequences, not by automatic deduction. A realization or interpretation can fail without the whole core failing. Operational fit also does not uniquely determine ontology: ontology may propose what exists, but representability or data-fit is not evidence of uniqueness.

Foundation

Definition. A system can investigate what it can access when it can register differences, retain traces for comparison, compose operations into procedures, and identify relations stable enough to test predictions.

Limitation. Conclusions established from inside the system must be tied to their operational scope, recording channels, preparation conditions, and corresponding errors. They do not automatically determine a unique structure for the whole beyond that scope.

Definition. Two models are operationally equivalent for a test scope when they predict the same distribution over every accessible record for every procedure in that scope. This neither asserts a shared ontology nor guarantees equivalence after the scope is enlarged.

Operational assumptions

OC-A1 — Distinguishability

Operational assumption. There is at least one procedure for which two situations produce outcomes, or distributions of records, distinguishable within a stated error model.

No complete-state readout, perfect single-shot discrimination, or absolute set of object-properties is assumed. Without a distinguishable difference, there is no data available to that observer for that comparison.

OC-A2 — Recordability and comparability

Operational assumption. Some traces persist long enough to enter comparisons, and criteria identify procedures as equivalent in the properties relevant to a test.

Memory need not be infallible, storage permanent, or a record complete. “This trace records that source” still needs a model and tests. Universal observer agreement is not assumed.

OC-A3 — Composability of procedures

Operational assumption. Some operations compose into structured procedures: preparation, interaction, readout, and comparison. Not every operation must compose with every other.

Foundational debt. Composition and retained traces already carry a primitive local ordering structure. The core does not derive all of time from a basis with no order relations. This local order alone supplies neither a universal linear order, standard duration, nor a thermodynamic arrow.

OC-A4 — Restricted regularity

Operational assumption. Within a specified domain, equivalent procedures exhibit statistical relations stable enough for calibration and prediction tests on further trials.

Determinism, IID trials, and laws invariant at every place and epoch are not assumed. Temperature, context, and preparation history belong in the model when they affect records. This is bounded induction, not a theorem about the whole universe.

Constraints on inference

OC-R1 — Predictive consistency

PM(r | Q)

Empirical constraint. Q is a specified procedure: relevant preparation conditions, operations, readout channels, and—where needed—a policy that selects later operations from earlier records. r is the complete accessible transcript, not only a final snapshot. M is the model.

Probabilities must be non-negative, normalized, and consistent under coarse-graining of outcomes for the same procedure. This does not impose a joint distribution on incompatible measurements or unobserved counterfactuals, and does not decide whether chance is ontologically fundamental.

OC-R2 — Representation invariance

Empirical constraint. Relabelling, changing units, or changing coordinates, with the model transformed consistently, must not alter predictions for the same physical procedure.

This is not the claim that every cut is equivalent. A change of description differs from a change of apparatus, access, or coupling. Descriptions of one measurement must agree; physically different measurements need not.

OC-R3 — Scope-limited identifiability

M1 ∼𝒬O M2  ⇔  PM1(r | Q) = PM2(r | Q)
for every Q ∈ 𝒬O and every accessible r

Definition. Keep three cases separate: current data have not distinguished the models; equivalence is proved for every procedure in a defined class; the models have the same ontology. The last does not follow from the first two.

Equal reduced states at one instant do not guarantee equivalence under every future interaction. Approximate equivalence must state a metric, tolerance, and experimental budget. More samples or an enlarged procedure class may reveal a sub-threshold difference. “Inaccessible here” is not “forever unknowable by humans,” and a finite numerical null is not an exact theorem about every test. For causal structure, compare model-level CM = (E, ⇝M) with the observer reconstruction ĈO from 𝒬O; absent detection does not prove absent model influence.

Model cut and operational access

Model cut

The way a model divides a system, its complement, and relevant interactions. In the current quantum realization it includes a chosen tensor factorization plus separately declared state, Hamiltonian split, locality, and coupling assumptions.

Operational access

The preparations, controls, readout channels, records, resolutions, and resources actually available to an observer. The class 𝒬O is generated from this access.

A cut is not redefined as a set of operations. A realization must explain how cut, state, coupling, and apparatus generate an access class. The map is not assumed one-to-one: one cut may support many access levels, and the same accessible statistics need not identify a unique cut. Operational indistinguishability also does not prove that no physical cut-selection principle can exist; any no-selection claim keeps the scope and assumptions of its own argument.

Limitation — no privilege outside the model. An endogenous test must declare its clock, memory, phase reference, preparation source, and signals. Simulator steps, the full state matrix, exact lattice coordinates, and debug variables belong to the model builder; they are not automatically records of the modeled observer.

Causal accessibility and scoped ordering

Model-level influence

For declared events or operational regions e and f in a physical realization M, write e ⇝M f when an admissible controlled change at e can alter the distribution of an accessible record at f, holding the remaining declared procedure fixed.

e ⇝M f ⇔ ∃ x, x′, Q, rf: PM(rf | do(x), Q) ≠ PM(rf | do(x′), Q)

x and x′ are intervention or preparation alternatives at e; Q fixes other conditions; rf is the record at f. Dynamics, localization, admissibility, and controls belong to the realization. A common-source correlation alone establishes no directed edge.

Model view · CM

Declared realization and admissible interventions determine ⇝M.

e → a → f
↘ b → f

Observer view · ĈO

Available detectors, interventions, clocks, references, records, resolution, and finite trials determine what can be reconstructed from 𝒬O.

e → a → f
⋯ b ⋯ f ?

OC-R3 application. In general ⇝𝒬O ≠ ⇝M. An undetected edge is not evidence of model-level absence; limited controls can also yield false positives. The cut and access class remain distinct. Compare CM = (E, ⇝M) with the reconstructed ĈO only under declared procedure and error rules.

Reachability. Write e ≺M f for a directed path of one or more model edges. If the domain is acyclic, this is a strict partial order (its reflexive closure is a partial order). With loops, it remains directed reachability. Acyclicity must be assumed or shown; unrelated events need no linear order. OC-A3 already admits primitive local sequencing, so this construction does not derive order from nothing.

Four tasks concerning time

These questions are related, but are neither an automatic sequence of deductions nor asserted to be fully independent.

TaskQuestion and required structureLimit
Ordering and influenceWhich stages can affect others? Local sequencing, interventions, dynamics, preparations, and controls yield causal reachability.No entropy monotonicity, absolute clock, or total order required.
Duration and clocksHow much calibrated change accumulates along a chain or worldline? Add physical reference, read/count rule, calibration, and comparison model.Order alone supplies no seconds or relativistic proper time.
Arrow and recordsWhy are histories and records asymmetric? Test boundary/preparation conditions, environment, coarse-graining, entropy, persistence, loss, and recoverability.Do not define the arrow by entropy increase then count that as an explanation.
Temporal judgement / experienceHow does an observer infer before/after, duration, and now? Model memory, integration, prediction, belief update, and report.Physical ordering does not require judgement or consciousness.

Keep distinct: model parameter t; causal reachability ≺M; reduced entropy SA; a calibrated physical clock reading; proper time in a suitable relativistic model; and τAent, a candidate entropic orientation label on a declared sufficiently monotone interval.

“Time as forgetting” is scoped to arrow and records. Asymmetric retention or loss of causal records may contribute to an embedded observer’s experienced arrow along an already ordered history. Entropy neither defines causal reachability nor supplies seconds. e₁ ≺ e₂ ≺ e₃ can hold even if SA(e₁) = SA(e₃) or entropy temporarily falls. Recurrence limits the entropic label; it does not erase causal order.

Worked examples

Clock matching without absolute time

Between two markers defined by a procedure, A records 100 ticks while B usually records 97. The relation can be calibrated, assigned an uncertainty, and tested on new trials. It does not show that A measures absolute time. If a change of description preserves all records and the 100:97 relation, OC-R2 forbids calling that relabelling a measured difference.

Entropy does not register every change

|ψ(φ)⟩ = (|0⟩ + e−iφ|1⟩) / √2

Every state in this family is pure and has entropy zero, but an appropriate relative-phase measurement can have probability cos²(φ/2). The measurement, phase reference, and preparation relation must be declared: this is not access to a global phase or a clock without additional structure. It is a counterexample to entropy as an indicator of every change, not a derivation of time's origin.

Penrose diagrams: model representation and access

A Penrose diagram compactifies a specified spacetime model. It preserves causal/conformal content such as null directions, reachability, horizons, and causal boundaries while discarding most metric scale. Finite placement of infinity, drawing distances, coordinate shape, and unconstrained visual angles are representational.

The full diagram is a model-level causal completion, not a literal picture seen by an embedded observer. That observer has signals, interventions, records, and local instruments and may reconstruct only part of the causal structure under its access class. See Sbierski’s General Relativity II notes, §3.2 for the conformal construction.

Limits and the current experiments

  • Global purity gives SU = 0; it does not imply stationarity or erase internal temporal relations. It only makes this global entropy a degenerate clock indicator.
  • Unitarity gives reversible evolution. Time-reversal symmetry is a separate condition.
  • A state and dynamics can determine the sign of entropy change; the entropy functional alone does not supply a universal macroscopic arrow.
  • A manifold or coordinate representation does not permit reversal of a physical history. A monotone index assumes or verifies monotonicity within a domain.
  • Formal resemblance between Shannon and von Neumann entropy does not identify uncertainty, information transfer, duration, and record order.
  • Recording does not always increase an observer's reduced-state entropy; preparation, correlations, environment, erasure, and resources matter.

The ruler-cancellation and orientation experiments retain their stored data, preregistered gates, and verdicts. Their conclusions apply to the declared models and access classes. The zero-coupling orientation null is not an ontological identity or a universal gauge theorem, and the tested crossover does not imply that every nonzero coupling reveals every distinction.

The 2026-09-22 follow-up compares access classes in the same declared realization: the original battery's weak-coupling orientation response is approximately quadratic, while an added pair-sensitive local reference gives an approximately linear response; the zero-coupling null remains. The reference, shared preparation phase, and joint readout are additional apparatus. The detectable leading response depends on access; this does not imply the underlying physical causality changes with measurement choice.

The current construction tracks two unresolved inputs, historically called “the two turtles”: arrow conditions and cut selection. This module neither proves that every theory has exactly two foundations nor closes cut selection, the arrow, or intersubjectivity. A future programme may enlarge procedure classes and compare realizations, but this revision introduces no new origin-of-time experiment.

Future work

A possible causal reconstruction pilot would use a local brickwork circuit or cellular automaton with a strict finite causal cone. Hide simulator steps and exact coordinates from the observer; permit tagged interventions and detector records. Expand access from restricted detectors to added references, an endogenous clock, then an asymmetric environment with persistent records. Compare model reachability CM = (E, ≺M) with ĈO. Detectable, missed, and false edges, reachability agreement, cone boundaries, and model uncertainty are possible future diagnostics; none is implemented here.
